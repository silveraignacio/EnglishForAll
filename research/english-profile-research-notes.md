## 1. What is English Profile?

English Profile ("English Profile – what the CEFR means for English") is a research programme
that explains what the **Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR)** means *specifically for
the English language*. The CEFR itself is **language-neutral** — it describes what learners
*can do* at six levels (A1–C2) but does **not** itemise the actual English words/grammar at each
level. English Profile fills that gap.

Key facts (from the "About / EP and the CEFR" pages):

- **Mission**: "provide a reliable and precise description of the English that learners know
  and use at each CEFR level" — i.e. **Reference Level Descriptions (RLDs) for English**.
- **Sponsorship**: Supported by the **Council of Europe**; research led by two University of
  Cambridge departments — **Cambridge University Press** and **Cambridge English Language
  Assessment** (now Cambridge University Press & Assessment).
- **Evidence base**: the **Cambridge Learner Corpus (CLC)** — hundreds of thousands of exam
  scripts from learners worldwide — plus the **Cambridge English Corpus** (multi-billion-word
  native corpus), exam vocabulary lists, classroom materials, course books, and reference lists
  for academic English / idioms at the C-levels.
- **Two flagship free online tools**:
  1. **English Vocabulary Profile (EVP) Online** — searchable database of words, meanings,
     phrases, phrasal verbs and idioms by CEFR level.
  2. **English Grammar Profile (EGP) Online** — searchable database of grammar "can-do"
     statements by CEFR level (1,222 entries total across A1–C2).
- Philosophy: **descriptive, not prescriptive** — it describes what learners *do* know, not what
  they *should* know. "It isn't meant to restrict teachers, but to help them by giving an
  indication of teaching and learning priorities." (EVP FAQ #6)

The CEFR levels map to three learner groups: **Basic User (A1, A2)**, **Independent User
(B1, B2)**, **Proficient User (C1, C2)**.

## 2. English Vocabulary Profile (EVP)

### How vocabulary is assigned to CEFR levels
- Each **word *and each separate meaning*** of a word is assigned a level. "Much more than a
  list of words" — it includes phrases, idioms, collocations and phrasal verbs.
- Assignment is **evidence-based**, triangulating:
  1. **Cambridge Learner Corpus** — when a word/meaning *begins to be used productively* by
     learners at a given level (receptive knowledge typically precedes productive, so the CLC
     pinpoints the productive onset).
  2. **Cambridge English Corpus** (native frequency) and other "general English" sources:
     exam vocabulary lists, classroom materials, a wide range of course books.
  3. For C-levels: academic-English reference lists and idiom frequency data.
- EVP reflects a **British and an American English version**, with **audio pronunciation** for
  all entries.

### Entry structure (EVP Online record)
Each entry displays, dictionary-style:
- **headword** (word / phrase / idiom / phrasal verb)
- **CEFR level** (A1–C2)
- **part of speech**
- **definition**
- **dictionary examples**
- **a typical learner example** (drawn from the CLC)
- **audio pronunciation**
- Advanced filters: **Topic**, **Part of speech**, **Grammar**, **Usage**, **Prefixes/Suffixes**
  (e.g. "find all –ness nouns for the topic 'People: Personality' → 26 hits, 11 at B2").

### Representative A1 vocabulary (categories/examples)
A1 vocabulary in the EVP is the "high-frequency, concrete, everyday" core. Typical A1
categories and examples (consistent with EVP A1 / standard CEFR A1 descriptors):
- **Greetings & social formulae**: *hello, hi, bye, goodbye, please, thank you, sorry, yes, no,
  OK, nice to meet you.*
- **Numbers, dates, time words**: *one…twenty, first/second, day, week, month, year, today,
  tomorrow, yesterday, now, morning, afternoon, evening, night, time, Monday…Sunday.*
- **Personal identification / family**: *name, age, man, woman, boy, girl, child, people,
  family, mother, father, mum/dad, brother, sister, son, daughter, parent, friend.*
- **Food & drink**: *food, drink, water, milk, tea, coffee, bread, apple, banana, rice, meat,
  fish, egg, eat, drink, hungry, thirsty.*
- **Common verbs (high-frequency)**: *be, have, do, go, get, come, make, like, want, know,
  think, see, look, say, live, work, play, read, write, eat, drink, buy, meet, call.*
- **Common adjectives**: *good, bad, big, small, new, old, hot, cold, happy, sad, nice,
  beautiful, easy, right, wrong, very + adjective.*
- **Question words**: *what, who, where, when, why, how, how old, how much/many.*
- **Pronouns/determiners**: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they; my, your, his, her, our; this;
  a/an/the; some, a lot of, lots of + numbers.*
- **Prepositions (simple)**: *in, on, at, to, from, with, for, of, about; here, there.*
- **House/everyday nouns**: *house, home, room, door, table, chair, school, shop, car, phone,
  book, pen, computer, money, job.*

> The EVP A1 set is deliberately **limited**: many "everyday" words a beginner might recognise
> (e.g. *kitchen, bathroom, weather, colour*) appear only at A2 in the EVP, and multi-meaning
> words are split (e.g. the A1 sense of *like* = verb of preference, vs. the preposition
> *like* at a higher level). Always check the specific sense/POS in EVP Online.

## 3. English Grammar Profile (EGP) — A1 specifically

### How grammar is assigned to CEFR levels
- EGP researchers analysed the **Cambridge Learner Corpus** to build detailed **"Can Do"
  statements** for grammatical **form** and **use** (and pragmatic appropriateness) at each
  level.
- Like vocabulary, **one grammatical form can have multiple meanings appearing at different
  levels** (the site's example: *may* — weak possibility, formal permission, *may well*, *may
  as well* — each at a different level).
- Each EGP entry has: **Super Category** (broad area, e.g. DETERMINERS, MODALITY) → **Sub
  Category** (e.g. articles, can) → **Level** (A1–C2) → **Can-Do statement** (FORM / USE /
  FORM/USE) → **Example** + learner example.
- Total EGP entries = **1,222**. With Level = A1, there are **109 entries** (the complete list,
  grouped by Super Category, is reproduced below).

### COMPLETE A1 grammar list (109 entries, from EGP Online, Level=A1)
Format: `SUB-CATEGORY — Can-do statement (FORM/USE)`.

**ADJECTIVES**

- modifying — WITH 'VERY' (FORM): use 'very' with common gradable adjectives.

**ADVERBS**

- … — PLACE (USE): *here, there* to indicate place.
- … — DEGREE WITH VERBS (USE): *really / very much* with *like, love* for emphasis.
- … — 'VERY' WITH ADJECTIVES (USE): *very* to make adjectives stronger.
- … — FREQUENCY (USE): *always, sometimes* for frequency.
- adverbs as modifiers — 'VERY' + ADJECTIVES (FORM).
- adverbs as modifiers — 'VERY' + TIME ADVERBS (FORM).
- adverbs as modifiers — PLACE, COMPLEMENT (FORM): *here, there* as complements of verbs.
- adverbs as modifiers — DEGREE and TIME, MODIFYING VERBS (FORM).
- adverb phrases - form — 'VERY' + ADVERB (FORM): modify time/degree adverbs with *very*.
- position — FRONT POSITION (FORM): a limited range of adverbs in front position.
- position — DEGREE, BEFORE ADJECTIVES (FORM): degree adverbs before an adjective.
- position — FREQUENCY, MID POSITION (FORM): frequency adverbs between subject and main verb.
- position — 'REALLY,' MID POSITION (FORM): *really* in mid position.
- position — TIME, END POSITION (FORM): time adverbs in end position.

**CLAUSES**

- declarative — AFFIRMATIVE DECLARATIVE (FORM): simple affirmative declarative clauses.
- declarative — NEGATIVE DECLARATIVE WITH 'BE' (FORM): negative with lexical *be* + *not/n't*.
- declarative — NEGATIVE DECLARATIVE, LEXICAL VERBS (FORM): simple negative declarative clauses.
- declarative — AFFIRMATIVE DECLARATIVE, MODAL AUXILIARY VERBS (FORM): affirmative declarative with modal verbs.
- interrogatives — AFFIRMATIVE INTERROGATIVE, WITH 'BE' (FORM): interrogative clauses with *be*.
- subordinated — 'BECAUSE', REASONS (FORM/USE): finite subordinate clause with *because*.

**CONJUNCTIONS**
- coordinating — PHRASE + PHRASE (FORM): *and, but, or* combine phrases.
- coordinating — SENTENCE + SENTENCE (FORM): *and, but, or* combine sentences.
- coordinating — LISTING (USE): *and/or* before the final list item.
- coordinating — CLAUSE + CLAUSE (FORM): *and, but, or* combine clauses.
- coordinating — WORD + WORD (FORM): *and, but, or* connect single nouns/adjectives.
- coordinating — 'BUT', CONCESSIVE (USE): *but* to add an unexpected contrast.
- subordinating — 'BECAUSE' (FORM): *because* as a subordinating conjunction.

**DETERMINERS**
- articles — WITH NOUNS (FORM): *the, a, an* before nouns.
- articles — 'A' + 'VERY' + ADJECTIVES (FORM): *a very …* in basic noun phrases.
- articles — 'A' + ADJECTIVES (FORM): *a/an* before adjectives in a noun phrase.
- articles — PREPOSITION + 'THE' + NOUN (FORM): *the* in prepositional time phrases.
- articles — PREPOSITION + NO ARTICLE (FORM): no article before some nouns in fixed expressions with prepositions.
- articles — NO ARTICLE (FORM/USE): no article before a limited range of nouns when referring to things in general.
- demonstratives — 'THIS', POINTING (USE): *this* to refer to places from the speaker's POV.  *(← ONLY 'this' is A1; 'that/these/those' are A2 — see §4)*
- demonstratives — 'THIS', FUTURE (USE): *this* with time/date words to mean 'the one coming'.
- demonstratives — 'THIS' WITH SINGULAR NOUNS (FORM): *this* with singular nouns.
- possessives — WITH NOUNS (FORM): possessive determiners **my, your, his, her, our** before nouns.  *(← NOT 'their' at A1; 'their' is B1, 'its' is B2)*
- quantity — WITH PLURAL NOUNS (FORM): *some, lots of, a lot of* + numbers with plural nouns.
- quantity — WITH SINGULAR NOUNS (FORM): *a, every* with singular nouns.

**FUTURE**

- can — OFFERS (USE): *can* to make an offer.

- negation — MAIN VERB 'BE' (FORM): negative of main-verb *be*, contracted/uncontracted.
- negation — MODAL VERB 'CAN' (FORM): *can't*.

**NOUNS**
- noun phrases — ADJECTIVE + PLURAL NOUN (FORM): adjective + plural noun, no determiner.
- noun phrases — DETERMINER + ADJECTIVE + NOUN (FORM): determiner + adjective + singular/plural noun.
- noun phrases — DETERMINER + NOUN (FORM): limited determiners + singular/plural nouns.
- noun phrases — NOUN + NOUN (FORM): pre-modify a limited range of nouns with another noun.
- types — PROPER NOUNS (FORM): capitalised proper nouns for people/places/days/things.
- types — COMMON NOUNS (FORM): a limited range of common nouns.
- types — COMPOUND NOUNS (FORM): one-word/two-word/hyphenated compounds (verb+noun, noun+noun, adj+noun).
- plural — PLURAL '-S' (FORM): plurals by adding *-s* to countable nouns.
- noun phrases - grammatical functions — AS SUBJECTS (FORM): nouns/NPs as subjects.
- … — AS COMPLEMENT OF PREPOSITIONS (FORM): nouns/NPs as complements of prepositions.
- … — AS OBJECTS (FORM): nouns/NPs as objects.
- … — AS ADJUNCTS (FORM): nouns/NPs as adjuncts in some time expressions.
- … — AS COMPLEMENT OF 'BE' (FORM): nouns/NPs as complements of *be*.

**PAST**
- past simple — AFFIRMATIVE (FORM): affirmative with a limited range of regular and irregular verbs.  *(← past-simple affirmative is A1; negatives & questions are A2 — see §4)*
- past simple — EVERYDAY EVENTS AND STATES (USE): past simple to talk about everyday events or states.

**PREPOSITIONS**
- prepositions — PREPOSITION + NOUN PHRASE (FORM): prepositional phrases (prep + NP).
- prepositions — SIMPLE (FORM): a limited range of simple single-word prepositions.  *(← 'prepositions of place' as commonly taught maps to this + the 'here/there' adverb A1 entries)*

**PRESENT**
- present continuous — AFFIRMATIVE (FORM): affirmative form.  *(← only affirmative is A1; negative & questions are A2)*
- present continuous — EVENTS IN PROGRESS (USE): present continuous with a limited range of verbs for situations in progress.
- present simple — NEGATIVE (FORM): negative with a limited range of regular/irregular verbs.
- present simple — AFFIRMATIVE (FORM): affirmative with a limited range of regular/irregular verbs.
- present simple — HABITS AND GENERAL FACTS (USE): present simple for repeated events/habits/general facts.
- present simple — WITH 'REALLY' (FORM): present simple with *really* as an intensifier.

> NOTE: present-simple QUESTIONS (do/does) do **not** appear as a separate A1 entry in the
> EGP set above — interrogatives at A1 are limited to "questions with BE" and "modal verbs
> yes/no". Present-simple *do/does* questions are generally treated as **A2** (compare the
> pattern: at A1 the EGP licenses negative via *don't*, but full *do/does* interrogatives are
> A2). Treat this boundary cautiously and verify against EGP Online if precision is required.

**PRONOUNS**
- subject/object — (SUBJECT) QUESTIONS WITH 'BE' (FORM): *you, it* after *be* in questions.
- subject/object — (OBJECT) 'IT' (FORM): *it* as direct object referring to an already-mentioned object/situation.
- subject/object — (OBJECT) WITH PREPOSITION (FORM): object pronouns *me, you, him, her, it, us, them* after prepositions.
- subject/object — (SUBJECT) QUESTIONS (FORM): *I, you, we, they* after an auxiliary/modal in questions.
- subject/object — (OBJECT) 'ME','YOU','HIM','HER','US','THEM' (FORM): object pronouns as direct object.
- subject/object — (SUBJECT) 'IT' FOR FIRST PERSON (FORM): *it* before *be* referring to a first-person speaker/writer.
- subject/object — (SUBJECT) STATEMENTS (FORM): subject pronouns *I, you, he, she, it, we, they* before a verb in statements.
- indefinite (thing/-one/-body etc) — OBJECT (FORM): a limited range of indefinite pronouns as objects.
- indefinite … — 'EVERYTHING', SUBJECT (FORM): *everything* as subject with a singular verb.

**QUESTIONS**
- yes/no — MODAL VERBS (FORM): a limited range of modal verbs + subject + main verb → yes/no questions (► *can*).

**VERBS**
- prepositional — VERB + PREPOSITION + OBJECT (FORM): a limited range of prepositional verbs + noun/pronoun object.
- types — AUXILIARY VERB 'BE' (FORM): auxiliary *be* (► present continuous).
- types — MODAL AUXILIARY VERBS (FORM): a limited range of modal auxiliaries — *can, will, would*.
- types — LINKING VERB, 'BE' (FORM): linking verb *be*.
- types — MAIN VERBS (FORM): regular and irregular main verbs in simple verb phrases (► present simple, past simple).
- patterns_with to and -ing — VERB + 'TO'-INFINITIVE (FORM): a limited range of verbs + to-infinitive.
- patterns_with to and -ing — 'WOULD LIKE TO' (FORM): *would like to* + infinitive.
- patterns_with to and -ing — 'LIKE' + 'TO'-INFINITIVE OR + '-ING' (FORM): *like* + to-inf or -ing, no meaning change.
- linking — 'BE' + COMPLEMENT (FORM): linking verb *be* with complements.
- there is/are — 'THERE IS' (FORM): *there is* + singular NP complement.
- there is/are — 'THERE ARE' (FORM): *there are* + plural NP complement.

**Practical A1 grammar syllabus (the EGP-licensed A1 core):**
- Subject & object pronouns; possessive determiners *my/your/his/her/our*; *this* (singular);
  articles *a/an/the* + no-article uses; plurals with *-s*; quantifiers *some/a lot of/lots of/
  numbers* + *a/every*.
- Verb *be*: affirmative, negative, questions; linking *be* + complement; *be* as auxiliary
  (→ present continuous affirmative); *there is/there are* (singular & plural).
- Present simple: affirmative + negative; habitual/general-fact use; with *really*.
- Present continuous: affirmative + 'events in progress' use (limited verb range).
- Past simple: affirmative (limited regular & irregular verbs) for everyday events/states.
- Modal *can*: affirmative, question, negative; uses — ability, requests, offers, possibility.
- *will/'ll*: affirmative; plans & intentions. *would like (to)* for wishes/invitations.
- Coordinating conjunctions *and/but/or*; subordinating *because*; simple coordinated clauses.
- Simple prepositions + prep+NP; place adverbs *here/there*; time/frequency/degree adverbs incl.
  *very, really, always, sometimes, soon*.
- Basic noun-phrase structure (determiner (+ adjective) + noun; noun+noun compounds; proper
  nouns); definite/indefinite/general reference.

**Deliberately A2 (not A1) — common surprises:**
- that / these / those (only *this* is A1).
- their / its (possessive *their* is B1, *its* is B2).
- present-simple *do/does* questions; present-continuous negatives & questions.
- past-simple negatives, questions, and *when*-clauses; fuller verb ranges.
- the *'s* possessive.
- imperatives (including *let's*) — **A2**, not A1.
- comparatives and regular superlatives.
- *be going to* (intentions/predictions) and *have (got) to* (obligation).
- *was/were*NEGATIVE/QUESTION forms (affirmative past of *be* is A1 via past-simple affirmative).

## 5. Pedagogical methodology (what English Profile recommends for A1)

English Profile is explicitly **descriptive, not a syllabus**. Direct guidance from the site:

- **Descriptive, not prescriptive** (EVP FAQ #6): "Should teachers only teach B1 words to B1
  students? — No… Students who have reached B1 should already know B1 words and would therefore
  benefit from exposure to B2 words." The programme "isn't meant to restrict teachers, but to
  help them by giving an indication of teaching and learning priorities."
- **Receptive vs productive** (EVP FAQ #1): EVP does not separate receptive from productive
  competence; "Productive skills usually follow receptive skills", and the CLC (productive
  evidence) plus native-frequency / input-text research are used together to pinpoint a word's
  level. Implication for teaching: A1 learners can productively *use* the A1 items; they can
  productively *attempt* (and receptively handle) some A2 items.
- **A1 ethos from the CEFR page**: A1 = *Basic User*. The "Introductory Guide to the CEFR"
  Global Scale describes A1 as handling concrete needs, simple phrases, basic personal and
  immediate-survival information, direct exchange, simple everyday interactions.
- Therefore the recommended **A1 teaching content** (synthesised from EVP/EGP and the CEFR
  descriptors):
  - High-frequency, **concrete** words for people, family, numbers/dates/time, food, body,
    house, school, common everyday activities.
  - A **limited range** of each grammar category (the EGP repeatedly says "a limited range
    of…") — emphasise form and the single most frequent meaning/use first; defer secondary
    meanings (e.g. *may* = weak possibility is B1+).
  - Prioritise the verb *be* (all persons, affirmative/negative/question), a limited set of
    common main verbs, present simple affirmative/negative, present continuous affirmative,
    past simple affirmative, *can* (ability/requests/offers/possibility), *there is/are*,
    basic noun-phrase and determiner patterns, and, but, or, because.
  - Keep A2 items (imperatives, *going to*, comparatives, *that/these/those*, *'s*) available
    for receptive exposure and as a natural stretch — but the EGP places **productive
    mastery** of them at A2.
- **Useful source articles** linked from "Other Resources":
  - Anne O'Keeffe & Geraldine Mark, "The English Grammar Profile of learner competence:
    Methodology and key findings," *International Journal of Corpus Linguistics* 22(4): 457–489.
  - "Introductory Guide to the CEFR" (free CUP PDF) — Global Scale table.
  - Annette Capel's *guide to using the English Vocabulary Profile* (YouTube).

## 6. Relationship / overlap with CEFR-J

CEFR-J (https://www.cefr-j.org/, Tono Laboratory, Tokyo University of Foreign Studies) is a
**separate, Japanese-led project** that is *not* hosted by English Profile. englishprofile.org
does *not* mention CEFR-J; cefr-j.org lists English Profile as a related external resource.
Both instantiate the CEFR for English but differ in granularity, evidence base and licensing.

| Aspect | English Profile (EVP / EGP) | CEFR-J |
|---|---|---|
| Levels | 6 CEFR levels: A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2 (single band per letter). | **12 subdivided levels**: Pre-A1, A1.1, A1.2, A1.3, A2.1, A2.2, B1.1, B1.2, B2.1, B2.2, then C1/C2 (C levels single). CAN-DO descriptors for 5 skills × 12 levels. |
| Aim | Reference Level Descriptions for English — *what English learners know/use at each CEFR level*. | Japanese-context CEFR instantiation — CAN-DO descriptors + supporting resources, validated with Japanese learners / Japanese education stage mapping. |
| Vocabulary source | Cambridge Learner Corpus (global) + Cambridge English Corpus (native) + exam/coursebook lists. | Coursebook corpus of major textbooks from **China / Korea / Taiwan** (Years 3–10), cross-referenced with the EVP; non-overlapping words added in as supplement → deliberately aligned to, but broader than, EVP. |
| Grammar source | EGP = CLC analysis → Can-Do statements (form/use). | CEFR-J Grammar Profile = grammatical-item frequency & **dispersion (range)** across **EFL coursebook corpus** + learner corpora (JEFLL written, NICT JLE spoken) + Japanese JHS/HS authorised textbooks. The "teacher version" uses **where in a coursebook** an item appears (front/middle/back) + frequency to recommend *when to introduce* each grammar item. |
| A1 vocabulary size | EVP A1 ≈ ~600 lemmas (approx.; verify in EVP Online). | **CEFR-J Wordlist A1 = 1,166 items (1,068 headwords)**; A2 = 1,411 (1,352 HW); B1 = 2,445 (2,353); B2 = 2,779 (2,692); total 7,801 / 6,868 headwords (Wordlist v1.6, 2020). CEFR-J A1 is markedly larger because it is textbook-driven. |
| Mapping to school stages | Tied to global CEFR; not tied to a national curriculum. | Explicitly mapped to **Japan's Course of Study**: A1 ≈ elementary–JHS Year 1; A2 ≈ JHS Y2–HS Y1; B1 ≈ HS Y2–university-entrance; B2 ≈ entrance–university general education. |
| Tools | EVP Online, EGP Online (registration/login). English Level Checker, CVLA text-level analyser are listed under CEFR-J instead. | CEFR-J Wordlist, CEFR-J Grammar Profile (PDFs + Perl regex extractor), CEFR-J Text Profile, CVLA text-level analyser, CAN-DO tests, ELP CAN-DO Descriptor Database. |
| **License / commercial use** | EVP data: **personal non-commercial research/teaching only**; commercial use / "English Profile informed" marketing needs **written consent** from CUP; mandatory acknowledgement. | **Free for research AND commercial use, with proper acknowledgement** (Tono Lab, TUFS). Citation: "The CEFR-J Wordlist Version 1.6. Compiled by Yukio Tono, Tokyo University of Foreign Studies. Retrieved from <URL> on <date>." (Editorial supervision for a commercial product may incur a fee — contact the lab.) Modifications are allowed **provided the original is cited**. |
| Existence of separate wordlist downloads | EVP is online-only (no bulk download; redistribution restricted). | Wordlist v1.6 downloadable as an Excel file with per-level sheets, POS, and thematic category tags (Core Inventory 1/2, Threshold notions). |

**Practical takeaway**: For an A1 course we *author ourselves*, CEFR-J's wordlist is the more
usable building block (free, downloadable, licensed for commercial use with attribution) and is
*aligned* with the EVP so its A1 band (~1,068 headwords) is a defensible superset of the EVP
A1 core. The EVP/EGP remain the authoritative *descriptive* reference for English learner
competence and should be cited (with the required acknowledgement) whenever their level
judgements are used; their raw data should not be copied into a commercial product.
